Beneish M-Score

Beneish Model (1999) that estimates the probability of earnings manipulation from eight accounting indices, calculated using the standardized financial statements (DFP) that companies submit to CVM. Alongside it, the accrual ratio measures how much of the earnings went through cash, and Altman's Z-Score measures insolvency risk.

⚠Educational and personal project, non-commercial. The model is a statistical screen, not an accusation: an M-Score above the threshold doesn't mean fraud, and the calculation may contain data or account-mapping errors. Nothing here is investment advice.

Method and limits

  • Data. Annual, consolidated standardized financial statements (DFP) from the CVM's Open Data Portal, for active listed companies classified as non-financial in the CVM registry (banks, insurers, and similar entities are excluded, because the model was estimated for industrial and commercial companies).
  • Model. Beneish's eight-variable M-Score (1999), with the coefficients from the original paper and a threshold of −1.78: above it, the company is classified as a possible manipulator.
  • Earnings Quality. Accrual ratio = (net income − operating cash flow) ÷ average total assets. The alert level combines this reading with the M-Score.
  • Missing data. When an account is not found, the collector uses zero; when an index cannot be calculated or comes out negative, it becomes 1 (neutral). When the requested fiscal year does not exist, the collector uses the closest one. Indices outside a wide range (above 10 or below 0.1; TATA beyond ±1) are also flagged, because they usually indicate a misclassified account or an atypical year. The fact sheets show these cases with ⚠, and the "complete data only" filter excludes them.
  • Altman Z-Score. Emerging markets version (Altman, Hartzell and Peck, 1995; Altman, 2005), which uses only accounting data: working capital, retained earnings (earnings reserves plus accumulated profits or losses), EBIT (income before financial result and taxes), and equity over total liabilities, all from the current fiscal year. EM score = 6.56·X1 + 3.26·X2 + 6.72·X3 + 1.05·X4 + 3.25. Above 5.85, safe zone; from 4.35 to 5.85, gray zone; below 4.35, risk. It measures insolvency risk, not manipulation: it complements the M-Score and does not factor into the alert level. The benchmarks come from U.S. companies and emerging-market bonds, not a Brazilian sample.
  • What the model does not measure. Changes in accounting standards, corporate reorganizations, sectors with their own cycle, and the context of each company. The model was estimated using American companies from the 1980 and 1990; applied to Brazil, it is a screening tool, not a diagnosis.
  • Events in documents (JEV). TypeSafe's System One model screens the titles of material facts and market communications from the last 12 months and, for those whose title points to some event, reads the document text to indicate whether they involve restatement of financial statements, audits, departure of the CFO or IR officer, investigations, debt restructuring, or a change in accounting policy. Only classifications with a confidence of at least 0.8 are included; generic titles are not sent. A document that is image-only, with no extractable text, keeps the title-based classification. This is supplementary information: it does not change the M-Score or the alert level.
  • Reference. Beneish, M. D. (1999). The Detection of Earnings Manipulation. Financial Analysts Journal, 55(5), 24 to 36.
