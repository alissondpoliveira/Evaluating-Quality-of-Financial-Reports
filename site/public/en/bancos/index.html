Banks and financial institutions: CAMELS signals

Beneish's M-Score doesn't work for banks: there's no inventory or gross margin, and the entry that most affects a bank's earnings is the provision for credit losses. This tab runs the equivalent screening for all institutions in the Central Bank's IF.data, using eight signals that compare the period with the same period of the prior year.

⚠Educational and personal project, non-commercial. It's a screen: a triggered signal points to where to look, it doesn't indicate fraud or risk of failure. The data is what institutions report to the Central Bank and may contain errors. Nothing here is investment advice.

Method and limits

  • Data. IF.data from the Central Bank: balance sheet, income statement, credit portfolio, and capital of Prudential Conglomerates and Independent Institutions, as of the latest reporting date published and the same reporting date of the prior year. Since January 2025, institutions have followed CMN Resolution 4,966 (expected loss, replacing the former provision by risk level), so the comparison only uses reporting dates under the new rule. In June and December, the income statement accumulates the half-year; in March and September, the quarter. Flow rates (ROE, cost of risk) appear annualized.
  • Why not the M-Score. Beneish's eight indices use sales revenue, gross margin, receivables, fixed assets, and depreciation, which don't describe a bank. The M-Score itself was estimated without financial institutions.
  • Where the signals come from. The financial institutions reading from the CFA curriculum (Level II, financial statement analysis) organizes the analysis by CAMELS (capital, asset quality, management, earnings, liquidity, and sensitivity to market risk) and points to credit loss estimates, fair value gains and other non-recurring income, and accelerated portfolio growth as risks to a bank's earnings quality. The signals translate this into period-over-period indices, in the format of the Beneish indices.
  • Two axes. The signals measure different things and are not added together. Earnings (IPR, ICO, VRN, ICC): results may be higher than they should be. Soundness (IAP, ICA, BPM, BAS): a transparent deterioration of credit or capital, which does not inflate earnings. Coverage falling together with problem assets rising counts as a single soundness event, because one drives the other almost by construction. This is the same logic as the companies site, which combines the M-Score with earnings quality.
  • Alert level. Critical: signal on both axes, earnings possibly inflated while risk rises, or capital below the minimum (Basel ratio below 10.5% or negative equity). Earnings to check: two or more earnings signals; in historical validation they anticipate a drop in earnings, not capital stress, hence the name. Attention: signals on one axis only. Normal: none. There are no coefficients: Beneish estimated his using a sample of companies that manipulated results, and there is no equivalent sample of Brazilian banks. The yardsticks for each signal are project choices, not values from the literature.
  • Peers and size. The profile shows, next to each index, the median of the evaluated institutions of the same type: a high index in a year when the whole type worsened carries less weight. Credit portfolios below R$ 10 million are left without an evaluation, because few contracts move the indices too much.
  • What the screening does not measure. Changes in strategy, acquisitions, portfolio sales, structured operations, cooperatives that pass funds on to their central cooperative, and the context of each institution. Institutions without a credit portfolio (brokerages, dealers, many payment institutions) appear with the snapshot, without an assessment.
  • References. CFA Institute, Level II, Financial Statement Analysis: Analysis of Financial Institutions. Beaver and Engel (1996) and Ahmed, Takeda, and Thomas (1999) on loan loss provisions as an earnings management tool.
